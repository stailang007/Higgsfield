## Chapter 1 — The Body Part Interrogators Watch First [blocks 3–6]
Joe Navarro spent twenty-five years as an FBI interrogator, and he built his entire method on one unglamorous body part, the feet. His reasoning was simple, people rehearse their faces for a lifetime, but almost nobody has ever practiced controlling where their feet point.

Navarro's rule, feet point toward what the mind wants, toward the exit when someone wants to leave, toward a person when someone is interested. Feet are honest because nobody rehearses them, the next signal is different, it can be faked for a moment, but never held for long.

## Chapter 2 — Eyes and the Reflex Underneath [blocks 7–11]
Eye contact is the most obvious social signal there is, which is exactly why it still leaks the truth people try hardest to hide. Two eye signals are genuinely reliable, a gaze that lasts longer than expected, and pupils that widen on their own when interest spikes.

But eye contact alone only proves interest, it doesn't prove the feeling is mutual, that's where a second signal, mirroring, comes in. Mirroring is a reflex, not a decision, it runs through the brain's mirror neuron system, wiring that fires just from watching someone move.

That's why real mirroring is almost impossible to fake for long, and why it can be interrupted in an instant by something even faster. [chapter closes on a doorway into microexpressions]

## Chapter 3 — The Flicker and What Lying Costs the Brain [blocks 12–15]
That something lasts less than a fifth of a second, psychologist Paul Ekman named it the microexpression, a flicker the conscious mind can't hide. Ekman insisted there's no single lying tell, deception shows up across five channels, the body, the face, the voice, the words, the story.

Two of those channels aren't the face at all, lying takes more mental effort than truth, and that effort leaks into speech and hands. Speech slows under that load, and the hands that normally gesture while speaking often go still the moment a story has to be invented.

## Chapter 4 — The One Rule That Actually Works [blocks 16–18]
None of these signals prove anything alone, what actually works is clusters, three or more signals pointing the same direction, close together in time. That's the discipline every real expert shares, Navarro with feet, Ekman with faces, never read one part of the body alone.

The body has been talking this whole time, most people never learned where to look, next time someone lies to you, watch their feet. [loop closed]
